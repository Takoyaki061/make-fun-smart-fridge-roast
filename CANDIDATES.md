## Critique and pick

Against the judge brief, in my own words:

1. (87) "My old fridge kept food cold for twenty years without once asking for my email address."
   Strongest. Roasts the smart fridge by contrast with the dumb one; the sting
   (an appliance demanding an email address) lands on first read; the
   account-signup angle is fresh — nobody roasts a fridge for wanting your
   inbox; specific (smart fridges really do demand app accounts); tight.
2. (97) "My fridge shows ads on its door screen now — it sells ad space on the one appliance I can't ad-block."
   Runner-up. Genuinely fresh angle (ads on the door), great sting
   ("can't ad-block"), but the laugh depends on knowing fridges show ads —
   less universal than #1's email-address absurdity.
3. (87) "The water dispenser has two settings: a trickle, and a fire hose aimed directly at your shirt."
   Tight and visual, real behaviour, but the laugh is slapstick rather than a
   sting, and it is about a dispenser, not the "smart" of the fridge.
4. (104) "My smart fridge emails me a monthly energy report, like a landlord who lives in my kitchen and charges rent."
   Specific and vivid (landlord image), but the punchline is a simile, not a
   turn — a smile, not a laugh.
5. (90) "My fridge told the oven I'm on a diet, and now the oven preheats out of spite."
   Charming cross-appliance gossip, fresh — but cute rather than funny, and the
   diet frame sits next to the banned diet-judging trope.
6. (105) "Vacation mode turns the fridge off to save power, then it emails me to ask why I abandoned it."
   Real behaviour (vacation mode), needy-fridge personification — but the sting
   is soft.
7. (118) "The fridge sent a push notification that the door was left open — to the phone in my hand, while I was holding it open."
   The most specific real behaviour of the set, but the longest, and the joke is
   the situation itself with no sharp turn at the end.

PICK: #1. Revised after the first local grade: "twenty years" -> "20 years"
(83 chars — punchier, and smaller text wins the tie-break). Final line:
"My old fridge kept food cold for 20 years without once asking for my email address."
